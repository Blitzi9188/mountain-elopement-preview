How to Plan Your Mountain Elopement

Planning an elopement is far simpler than a big wedding — but a few decisions early on make everything flow. Here is the short version.

At a glance
Lead time
3–9 months
Guests
0–20
Includes
Photo · Film · Planning

1 · Choose the feeling, then the place

Do you want adventure and effort, or calm and ease? That answer points us to the right region and location.

2 · Pick a season and a date range

We build in a weather buffer so we can move your day by a few hours or a day for the best conditions.

3 · Leave the rest to us

Permits, timeline, flowers, hair & make-up, transfers and the legal path — all handled with our partners.

Permits, access & the small costs

Beyond the big line items, a mountain day has small, easy-to-miss costs. Some locations need a photography or ceremony permit; the Tre Cime di Lavaredo toll road runs about €30–45 per car; Lago di Braies has a summer access window and paid parking; cable-car tickets and a night in a rifugio add up too. None of it is expensive — it just needs planning, which we do for you. The ones nobody warns you about are in what nobody tells you about eloping.

A day that never feels rushed

A typical sunrise day starts in the dark: hair and make-up, a short drive or lift, then vows as the first light hits the rock. Portraits follow while the trails are still empty, then a long breakfast at a hut and time simply to be married. We build in a buffer — often a spare day — so weather never forces a rushed timeline. Deciding between first and last light? Read sunrise or sunset?, or see a full unhurried day in this three-day Italian elopement.

How far ahead to book

Popular summer dates fill 6–12 months out; the off-season is often possible on shorter notice.

Good to know

  • Decide the feeling before the location.
  • Build in a weather buffer day.
  • Let us handle permits and vendors.
